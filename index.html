Lab 3 · How to use this worksheet

The starting sheet supplies labels and units only. All measurement, constant and formula cells are yours to fill. Use this app for your data, calculations and graphs. Enter written explanations directly in Gradescope.

1 · Enter and edit a cell
  1. Click a cell. Its address, such as A3, appears beside the formula bar. Letters identify columns; numbers identify rows.
  2. Type a measurement or label. Enter numbers without units, for example 50. Put units in a heading or separate units row.
  3. Press Enter to save the entry and move down, or Tab to move right. Clicking another cell also saves the entry.
  4. To edit a cell, click it and change its entry. You can also use the ƒx formula bar above the grid, then press Enter or click Apply. Press Escape in a cell to cancel an edit.

The grid displays a formula’s result after you leave the cell. Clicking it shows the original formula; the formula bar and cell tooltip also let you inspect the calculation.

2 · Write your first formula

Every formula starts with =. Use cell addresses for the values you need; you do not need to type ℓ, δ or other mathematical symbols.

If A3 contains a mass in grams, click the cell where you want the mass in kilograms, type =A3/1000, and press Enter. If A3 is 50, the result is 0.05.

Changing A3 later updates every calculation that refers to it. Keep conversions in formulas, rather than retyping rounded results.

OperationExample
Add / subtract=F3-J3
Multiply=E3*F3
Divide=A3/1000
Square=E3^2
Group operations=(B15-B21*B14)/B13

Use * for multiplication, / for division, ^ for powers, and ordinary parentheses. Do not enter ×, ÷, superscript digits or units inside a formula.

3 · SUM, COUNT, SQRT and other functions

A function name is followed by parentheses. A colon means “through”: E3:E8 includes E3, E4, E5, E6, E7 and E8.

FormulaWhat it does
=SUM(E3:E8)Adds the six cells.
=COUNT(E3:E8)Counts numeric entries, including zero.
=AVERAGE(E3:E8)Finds the mean of numeric entries.
=SQRT(B23/(B13-2))Takes the square root of the expression inside.
=ABS(B21)Returns a value’s magnitude.

SUM, COUNT and AVERAGE accept one cell or one rectangular range. For separate ranges, use =SUM(E3:E5)+SUM(E7:E8). These functions ignore blank cells and text in a range, but errors in formula cells remain errors. Include all your intended primary readings; exclude headers, summary cells and repeat/check readings from fit ranges.

Function names are case-insensitive: SUM and sum both work. This app supports the functions listed here, rather than the full Google Sheets function library.

4 · Fill a formula down a column
  1. Enter the formula in the first data row, for example =A3/1000 in D3. Press Enter.
  2. Click D3, then hold Shift and click D8. The selected rectangle should read D3:D8 beside the formula bar.
  3. Click Fill down. D4 receives =A4/1000, D5 receives =A5/1000, and so on.

Fill down repeats the top selected row. It replaces entries below that row after confirmation when those cells are occupied. You can fill several adjacent columns together.

5 · Keep a reference fixed with $

A normal reference changes when you fill down. An absolute reference stays fixed.

ReferenceBehavior when copied
E3Row and column may change.
$B$22Always refers to B22.
B$22Row 22 stays fixed.
$B22Column B stays fixed.

For example, if your intercept A is in B22, slope B is in B21, and first force is in E3, use =$B$22+$B$21*E3 for predicted position. Filling down changes E3 to E4 but keeps the same fitted A and B.

All cell locations in this guide are examples. Use the addresses in your own worksheet. They match the layout loaded by Load completed example.

6 · Build the Lab 3 calculation

Column and summary labels are supplied; you may edit them. Enter your original measurements, then write each calculation formula yourself. Convert masses to kg and positions and position uncertainties to m. Calculate F = mg using exact g = 9.8 m/s², then F² and Fℓ.

In your summary area, calculate N and the sums of F, ℓ, F² and Fℓ. Translate these expressions into cell references:

x = F, y = ℓ
D = NΣF² − (ΣF)²
B = (NΣFℓ − ΣFΣℓ)/D
A = (Σℓ − BΣF)/N
ℓ̂ᵢ = A + BFᵢ
rᵢ = ℓᵢ − ℓ̂ᵢ
S = Σrᵢ²
s = √[S/(N−2)]
δ_A = s√(ΣF²/D)
δ_B = s√(N/D)
k = 1/B
δ_k ≈ δ_B/B²

Fit uncertainties use residual scatter s, distinct from your measurement uncertainty δᵢ. Keep full precision in your cells; round the final reported result as instructed in Lab 3.

Check calculation compares the result cells you specify with OLS. It gives feedback without replacing your formulas and does not check your unit conversions.

7 · Select, paste and copy

Click a cell and Shift-click a second cell to select a rectangle. Click a column letter to select a column; Shift-click another letter to select several columns.

Copy a rectangular block in another spreadsheet, click its destination cell here, and paste. The block fills cells to the right and below; pasted values replace existing entries in that area. Labels and formulas can be pasted too.

Use Copy selection to copy displayed results as a table. Use CSV export for both displayed values and a separate record of original entries and formulas.

8 · Create your graphs
  1. Select your force and position columns, then click Create graph. You can also choose columns in the dialog.
  2. Set First data row and Last data row to the primary measurements. Choose the SI position-uncertainty column for vertical error bars.
  3. Enter the title and axis labels with units. To add your calculated straight line, enter the addresses of your A and B cells.
  4. Create a second graph with force horizontally and residual vertically. Leave A and B blank and check Add zero reference line.

Open Edit graph labels, data and axes to adjust bounds or tick spacing. Blank bounds use automatic scaling. Parameter-uncertainty cells can be added to the line caption there. Error bars do not weight the OLS fit.

9 · Save and export for Gradescope

The app autosaves in this browser. Click Save to download an editable JSON backup and Open to restore one. Browser storage is not a portable backup.

Click Export for the table and formula CSV or Preview / print analysis. From the preview, print to PDF using your browser’s print dialog. Each graph also has SVG and PNG export buttons.

Submit the required tables, calculations and graphs to Gradescope. Enter procedural explanations and interpretation there.

Load completed example supplies artificial measurements and a finished numerical analysis for demonstration. Restore previous sheet returns to the saved pre-example sheet.

10 · Fix a formula error

Click a cell showing #ERROR. Read the message in the status bar below the grid or the cell tooltip.

  • Missing value: a directly referenced cell is blank. Enter the needed value or correct the reference.
  • Text in a cell: a formula references a heading or a number with units typed into it. Reference the numeric data cell.
  • Circular reference: the calculation eventually refers back to itself. Check its dependencies.
  • Result is not finite: check for division by zero or a square root of a negative number.
  • Unknown name / missing parenthesis: check spelling, parentheses, multiplication signs and cell addresses.

For example, enter =SQRT(B23/(B13-2)) with both closing parentheses. For OLS uncertainty calculations, N must exceed 2 and the force values must vary.
